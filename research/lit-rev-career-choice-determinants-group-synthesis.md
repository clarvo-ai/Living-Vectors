# Literature Review on Career Choice Determinants

## Summary

**Employee retention and performance are driven more by person–organization fit than by skills or compensation alone. Clear and practiced company values, aligned culture, and realistic career development opportunities significantly reduce employee turnover and increase satisfaction. Compensation matters, but its impact depends strongly on risk tolerance and how pay is structured rather than on salary level alone. Mismatches in values, expectations, or risk preferences are a major cause of regret, disengagement, and turnover.**

## I. Culture Match & Person-Organization Fit

### Key Research Findings

- Person–Organization Fit (POF) has a **stronger impact on retention and satisfaction** than Person–Job Fit (PJF).
- When company values are clear and actually practiced, employees feel **trust and "fit"**, which increases engagement, performance, and retention.
- Value mismatch leads to **lower satisfaction and regret** after job changes.
- Strong values are linked to higher engagement and lower turnover.
- Organizational culture affects performance **directly**, not just through satisfaction.
- Replacing an employee costs **1.5–2× annual salary**, so poor fit is expensive.

### What This Means for Living Vectors

- POF (Person-organization-fit) should be **weighted higher than PJF** (Person-job-fit) when matching people to jobs.
- Go beyond skills → match on:
  - Values (autonomy vs structure, feedback style)
  - Culture (innovation, stability, hierarchy tolerance)
  - Organizational climate preferences
- Use culture signals early to reduce employee turnover before it happens. This means identifying culture fit before the hire or match is finalized, not after problems appear.

## II. Values Ranking & Alignment

### Key Research Findings

- Values alignment explains **why people stay**, even when pay is not perfect.
- Employees evaluate culture before applying.
- Value-driven recognition increases motivation, performance, and long-term commitment.
- Culture types matter (Competing Values Framework):
  - Clan (people-focused)
  - Hierarchy (structure, stability)
  - Market (results, competition)
  - Adhocracy (innovation, autonomy)
- Misaligned expectations (not skills) often cause dissatisfaction.

### What This Means for Living Vectors

- Use the **Competing Values Framework (CVF)** as a matching dimension.
- Ask the AI to **rank users across CVF dimensions**, rather than assigning a single fixed type.
- Expand the question set to capture value trade-offs:
  - Autonomy vs structure
  - Innovation vs predictability
  - Feedback style
  - Growth vs stability
- Model values dynamically (not one fixed "ideal profile").
- Use short, value-based questions instead of generic personality tests or skill assessments.

## III. Compensation & Risk Tolerance

### Key Research Findings

- Research on compensation shows **mixed and sometimes contradictory results**:
  - Some studies identify pay as a major driver of turnover.
  - Others find salary increases have **little or no effect** on long-term retention.
- A consistent pattern is that pay alone is **insufficient without growth, management quality, and culture**.
- Compensation has two dimensions:
  - **Monetary:** salary, bonuses, incentives
  - **Psychological:** growth, recognition, meaning
- Risk attitude strongly affects pay preference:
  - Risk-averse → prefer fixed pay, structure, predictability
  - Risk-tolerant → prefer variable pay, autonomy, fast change
- Higher risk aversion = lower likelihood of performance-based pay.
- Matching pay structure to risk profile improves productivity and reduces stress.

### What This Means for Living Vectors

- Treat **risk tolerance as a core matching signal**.
- Match:
  - Risk-averse users → stable roles, fixed salary, clear structure
  - Risk-tolerant users → startups, sales, performance-based pay
- Use **structured learning** for compensation preferences:
  - Ask users to rank what matters more: base pay, bonuses, recognition, growth
  - Let the AI extract and store these priorities as structured signals
- Avoid one-size-fits-all pay assumptions.
- **(!!)** Most studies on pay and compensation referenced here are based on the US job market (2021–2022). Cultural, geographical, regulatory, and economic differences may limit how directly these findings apply to other contexts. Compensation signals should therefore be interpreted alongside local norms and user-specific preferences.

## IV. Career Development & Retention Signals

### Key Research Findings

- Lack of growth opportunities is a **major cause of turnover**.
- Employees stay longer when they see the company as a place to reach long-term goals.
- Career development beats salary when deciding whether to accept a job.
- Management quality strongly predicts retention.
- Work–life balance preferences differ by demographics but matter across the board.

### What This Means for Living Vectors

- Ask about:
  - Long-term career goals
  - Learning & advancement expectations
  - Preferred manager style
  - Work–life balance needs
- Match candidates to employers who can realistically support their growth path.
- Use satisfaction signals to predict whether someone can "survive" a mismatch short-term.

## V. Key Takeaways Summarized

### What Repeats Across Studies

- Fit > Pay (in the long run).
- Values alignment reduces regret and employee turnover.
- Compensation works best when combined with growth and culture.
- Risk tolerance is underrated but highly predictive.
- Misalignment is often **non-linear**: small mismatches can cause big drops in performance.
